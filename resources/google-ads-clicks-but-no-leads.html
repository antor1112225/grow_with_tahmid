The problem

Your Google Ads dashboard shows clicks every day and the budget is being spent, but the phone isn't ringing and the inbox is empty. You can't tell whether the ads are broken, the targeting is wrong, or Google Ads simply doesn't work for your business.

Where this comes up: Reddit r/PPC, Facebook groups and client questions

Quick answer

When Google Ads gets clicks but no leads, the cause is usually one of five things: broken or missing conversion tracking, clicks from irrelevant search terms, clicks from outside your service area, budget leaking into Display, Search Partners or Performance Max placements, or a landing page that makes it hard to contact you. Check them in that order. Tracking comes first because if leads are arriving but not being recorded, every other decision you make will be based on wrong data.

Diagnose clicks without leads in the right order

When I audit an account with this problem, I resist the urge to start rewriting ads or changing bids. Most "no leads" accounts have more than one issue, and fixing them out of order wastes time and money. I work through the funnel from measurement to the conversation with the customer:

  1. Measurement – are leads actually missing, or just not being recorded?
  2. Traffic quality – which searches are triggering your ads?
  3. Geography – are the people clicking actually in your service area?
  4. Inventory – where is the budget really being spent (Search, Display, Search Partners, Performance Max)?
  5. Message match – does the ad promise what the business actually offers?
  6. Landing page – can a ready-to-buy visitor contact you in a few seconds?
  7. Lead handling – when someone does call or submit a form, does anyone respond?

Each layer depends on the one before it. There is no point optimizing a landing page for traffic that was never going to buy, and no point judging traffic quality if your conversion data is wrong.

Confirm your conversion tracking is actually recording leads

A surprising number of "no leads" accounts are actually "no tracked leads" accounts. Before anything else, compare what Google Ads reports with what the business actually received over the same date range: calls in the phone system, form emails, CRM entries and booking notifications.

What to check

  • Conversion actions: in Goals > Conversions, check that each action shows a recent status of "Recording conversions" rather than "Inactive" or "No recent conversions".
  • Primary vs secondary: only primary actions are used for bidding and appear in the main Conversions column. A correctly firing form action set to secondary will look like zero leads.
  • Form tracking method: if the tag fires on a thank-you page, make sure the form still redirects there. Many form plugins switch to inline "success" messages after an update, which silently breaks page-view based tracking.
  • Calls: calls from call assets need a "Calls from ads" conversion action, and calls from the website need a Google forwarding number or a call tracking tool. Clicking a tel: link is not the same as a completed call.
  • Tag Assistant: submit a test lead with Google Tag Assistant open and confirm the conversion event fires once, not zero times or three times.

If real leads are arriving but not being recorded, fix that before changing anything else. Smart Bidding strategies like Maximize Conversions are flying blind without it. My guide to common Google Ads conversion tracking problems covers the usual failure points in more detail.

Check whether the clicks are coming from the right searches

Keywords are what you bid on; search terms are what people actually typed. Open the Search terms report (Insights and reports > Search terms) for the last 30 to 90 days, sort by cost, and read the top terms one by one.

For local service businesses, the same patterns of wasted clicks show up again and again:

  • Job seekers: for example, a plumber bidding on broad match "plumbing" can end up paying for "plumbing jobs near me" or "plumber apprentice salary".
  • DIY researchers: "how to fix a leaking tap" or "how to recharge car AC myself" signal someone who wants to avoid hiring you.
  • Wrong service: a roofer showing for "roof rack installation", or a detailer showing for "car wash tokens".
  • Competitor and brand navigation: people searching for a specific company's login page or phone number.
  • Information and pricing research far from purchase: "average cost of a personal injury settlement" is not the same as "personal injury lawyer near me".

How to fix it

  • Add irrelevant terms as negative keywords, and build a shared negative keyword list for recurring themes (jobs, salary, training, free, DIY, how to).
  • Tighten match types on your highest-spend keywords if broad match keeps drifting. I compare the trade-offs in broad match vs phrase match for local services.
  • Check the share of spend with "Other search terms" hidden from the report. If it is large, judge quality by downstream lead data rather than the visible terms alone.

Check whether people clicking are actually in your service area

This is one of the most common causes of clicks with no leads for local businesses, and it is easy to miss because the campaign settings look correct at a glance.

In campaign settings, under Locations > Location options, Google Ads defaults to "Presence or interest": people in, regularly in, or who have shown interest in your targeted locations. That means someone in another state reading about your city can see your ads. For a business that only serves a 30-mile radius, those clicks will almost never convert.

  • Switch to "Presence: People in or regularly in your included locations" for local service campaigns.
  • Review the Locations report using the "Where users were" (matched location) view to see where clicks physically came from.
  • Exclude areas you don't serve, even if they sit inside a larger targeted region.
  • Check that radius targeting reflects where you will actually travel, not a generous guess.

Check where your budget is really being spent

A "Search" campaign is not always only Google Search. When a campaign is created, the Google Search Partners and Display Network options are often enabled by default. Search Partners can be fine, but Display expansion inside a Search campaign tends to produce cheap clicks with low intent.

  • Segment campaign performance by Network to see how much spend went to Search Partners and Display.
  • In campaign settings, untick "Include Google Display Network" for lead-focused search campaigns. Test Search Partners separately rather than leaving it on by default.
  • If you run Performance Max, review the channel performance and search term insights, add brand exclusions where appropriate, and use campaign-level negative keywords. PMax optimizes toward whatever you count as a conversion, so weak tracking plus PMax often means lots of low-quality traffic.

As a rule, I want a new local lead-generation account to prove itself on tightly controlled Search campaigns before expanding into broader inventory.

Check whether your ads attract the wrong expectations

Sometimes the searches are right but the ad sets the wrong expectations. An ad that says "Cheap Plumbers – Call Now" attracts price shoppers; an ad that is vague about service area brings in people you can't serve.

  • Mention the service area and the specific service in headlines ("Emergency Furnace Repair in Tulsa").
  • Qualify where it matters: "Free consultation for injury cases", "Ceramic coating from $X", "Residential only".
  • Add call assets with call reporting and schedule them for hours when someone will answer.
  • Use sitelinks that send people to the specific service pages they care about, not just the homepage.

Check whether the landing page makes it easy to contact you

If the search terms and locations look right but conversion rate is still near zero, the landing page is the next suspect. Open it on your phone, on mobile data, and look at it like a stranger who needs the service today.

  • Is the phone number visible and tappable without scrolling?
  • Does the headline match the search? Someone searching "water heater replacement" should not land on a generic homepage about "quality home solutions".
  • Is the form short? Name, phone, service and postcode is often enough for a first contact.
  • Is there trust evidence? Reviews, licenses, photos of real work, guarantees.
  • Does the page load quickly and actually work? Test-submit the form and confirm the notification arrives.

For example, imagine a detailing business sending ad clicks to a booking page that asks visitors to create an account before seeing prices. Plenty of people will click; very few will finish.

Check what happens when someone does try to reach you

The last layer sits outside Google Ads entirely. Leads can exist and still be "lost" if nobody picks up.

  • Listen to a sample of call recordings, or review the call log for missed and short calls during ad hours.
  • Check that form notifications aren't landing in spam or going to an old email address.
  • Measure response time. A lead who waits until tomorrow for a callback has often already booked someone else.
  • Align ad schedules with staffed hours, or route after-hours calls to a service that answers.

When to get an outside audit

If you've worked through every layer and still can't find the leak, a fresh set of eyes on the account, tracking and landing page usually finds it faster than more trial and error. Quick diagnostic checklist

  • Compare Google Ads conversions with real calls, forms and CRM entries for the same dates
  • Confirm lead conversion actions are primary and "Recording conversions"
  • Submit a test lead and verify the tag fires exactly once
  • Review the search terms report by cost and add negative keywords
  • Set location options to "Presence" and review where users actually were
  • Segment by network and switch off Display expansion in Search campaigns
  • Check that the landing page matches the search and shows a tappable phone number
  • Audit missed calls, spam-filtered form emails and response times

Frequently asked questions

How many clicks should I get before expecting a lead from Google Ads?

It depends on your conversion rate, which varies widely by industry, offer and landing page. Rather than waiting for a magic number, review search terms and location data early. If the traffic is clearly relevant and you still see no leads after a meaningful number of clicks, the landing page or lead handling is the likely problem.

Can Google Ads show clicks that are not real people?

Google filters a lot of invalid traffic automatically and credits some of it back, which you can see in the invalid clicks column. Bot and accidental clicks are more common on Display and some Performance Max placements than on Google Search itself. Keeping lead campaigns focused on Search reduces exposure.

Should I switch from Maximize Clicks to Maximize Conversions?

Only once conversion tracking is accurate and recording real leads. Maximize Clicks optimizes for volume of clicks, not leads, so it often finds cheap, low-intent traffic. Maximize Conversions needs reliable conversion data to work well.

Is it my landing page or my targeting?

Look at the search terms and location reports first. If they show relevant searches from your service area, targeting is probably fine and the landing page or offer is the likelier cause. If they show irrelevant searches or out-of-area clicks, fix targeting before redesigning the page.
