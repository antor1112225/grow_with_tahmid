How I work

A transparent four-step process

Tracking first, then campaigns, then disciplined optimization toward qualified leads.

  1. 01

    Discovery & Research

    We define what a qualified lead is worth to you, then I research search demand, competitors, your service area and your current account to find where money is leaking.

    OutcomeLead definition, budget estimate, audit findings

  2. 02

    Tracking & Campaign Setup

    Calls, forms and bookings are tracked as separate, verified conversions before a single dollar is spent. Campaigns are built around intent, location and offer.

    OutcomeVerified tracking, campaign structure, ads & landing page plan

  3. 03

    Launch & Data Collection

    Campaigns go live with controlled budgets. I watch search terms, lead quality and call recordings closely and remove waste early, while the platforms gather learning data.

    OutcomeLive campaigns, early waste removed, first lead-quality feedback

  4. 04

    Optimization & Scaling

    Budget shifts toward what produces qualified leads and booked jobs. Winning campaigns are scaled carefully; offline conversions teach bidding what a good lead looks like.

    OutcomeLower cost per qualified lead, documented changes, monthly review

Should I run Google Ads or Meta Ads for lead generation?

Google Ads captures people who are already searching for your service, so it usually produces higher-intent leads faster. Meta Ads creates demand among people who are not searching yet, which works well for visual, considered or seasonal offers. Many businesses start with Google and add Meta once tracking and follow-up are solid.

How do Meta Ads leads differ from Google Ads leads?

Meta leads are often cheaper but less intent-driven, because the person was scrolling rather than searching. Quality improves with a specific offer, qualifying questions, higher-intent form settings, fast follow-up and sending real lead outcomes back to Meta through the Conversions API.

What makes a lead “qualified”?

We agree on a definition before launch: someone in your service area, needing a service you offer, with a realistic timeline and budget. That definition shapes the targeting, the form questions, the tracking and the way campaigns are optimized.

How much should I spend on ads?

It depends on your market, your cost per click and the value of a customer. During the strategy call I estimate a realistic starting budget from keyword and competition data, so you know what volume of leads to expect before committing.

Why does conversion tracking matter so much?

Google and Meta optimize toward whatever you tell them is a conversion. If page views or duplicate events are counted as leads, the algorithm learns the wrong thing and spends money finding more of it. Accurate call, form and booking tracking is the foundation of every account I manage.

How do you improve lead quality, not just lead volume?

By filtering intent at every step: search-term and negative keyword control, location targeting, pre-qualifying ad copy, form questions, and importing qualified or closed leads from your CRM so bidding favours the people who actually become customers.

How long until I see results?

Search campaigns can produce leads within the first days, but the first 4–8 weeks are mainly about collecting data and removing waste. Most accounts become steadier and more efficient after two to three months of consistent optimization.

What does working together look like?

It starts with a free strategy call and an audit of your current setup. If we are a good fit, I set up tracking and campaigns, launch, and then optimize weekly. You get clear reporting on spend, leads and cost per lead, and you keep ownership of every account.
